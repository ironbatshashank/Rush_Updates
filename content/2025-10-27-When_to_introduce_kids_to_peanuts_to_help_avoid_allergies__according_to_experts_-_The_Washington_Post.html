Washington Post: Experts Urge Early Peanut Introduction to Combat Allergies

Worried about peanut allergies in your child? New guidelines, highlighted by The Washington Post, confirm that introducing peanuts to infants as early as 4 to 6 months can significantly reduce allergy risk. Experts now strongly recommend this proactive approach for most babies.

Historically, parents were advised to delay allergen introduction. However, groundbreaking research, including the LEAP study, demonstrates that early, regular exposure helps build tolerance. For babies without existing food allergies or severe eczema, age-appropriate peanut products – like smooth peanut butter thinned with water or infant peanut puff snacks – are encouraged around 4-6 months. High-risk infants may even start earlier, often with medical guidance. This simple step is a powerful strategy in preventing potentially life-threatening peanut allergies, offering parents greater peace of mind.
